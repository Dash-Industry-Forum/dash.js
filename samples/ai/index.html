ML-based client-side upscaling

SVTA Hackathon · Player Intelligence dash.js samples
The samples need a browser with WebGPU (e.g. Chrome or Edge). Costs below were measured on an Apple GPU in Chrome.
Concept
dash.js deliberately fetches a low-resolution rendition (320x180, 200 kbps) and upscales it 4x to 1280x720 on the device with WebGPU. Super-resolution models recover detail, so the viewer gets a sharper picture while much less bandwidth is used. Only the player can make this trade between bandwidth and on-device compute. Everything runs in the browser and frames never leave the GPU; each step is an isolated sample that uses only the public dash.js API.
Step 1a · Baseline
WebGPU upscaling (passthrough)
Plain bilinear upscaling in a WebGPU shader. Proves the frame pipeline and sets the baseline cost.
Method
Bilinear ×4
Cost
~3 ms/frame
Result
Same as the original; difference view is flat grey

Step 1b · Super-resolution
ESPCN super-resolution
ESPCN x4, a small 3-layer CNN, written by hand as WebGPU compute shaders. No ML runtime.
Method
ESPCN ×4 (luma), OpenCV weights
Cost
~7–10 ms/frame
Result
Slightly crisper edges; compression artifacts stay
Open sample
Step 1c · Super-resolution
Real-ESRGAN animevideov3
Compact Real-ESRGAN video model trained on realistic degradations, run by onnxruntime-web on WebGPU.
Method
Real-ESRGAN ×4 (RGB), official weights
Cost
fp32 ~65 ms/frame (~16 SR fps), fp16 ~48 ms/frame (~22 SR fps)
Result
Clearly sharper; compression blur and blocking removed
Open sample
Step 2 · Planned
SR-aware quality selection
While upscaling is active, dash.js caps the bitrate at the lowest rendition the model can upscale to the display size, and shows the bandwidth saved.
Step 3 · Planned
Energy-aware upscaling
The upscaling level (Real-ESRGAN, ESPCN, off) adapts to frame cost, CPU pressure, battery and dropped frames. When it steps down, the bitrate cap lifts.
In every sample
  • Original <video> and upscaled canvas side by side, both at the exact output size (one canvas pixel per screen pixel).
  • Overlays with decoded and output resolution, upscaling method and ms/frame.
  • Difference ×10: what the upscaler adds compared with bilinear (grey = nothing). Zoom 4×: both panels magnified at the cursor.
  • Content selector: Envivio (live action, default) and Big Buck Bunny (animation), both played from their 320x180 rendition.
